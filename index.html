01 · Executive Summary

Delivery is running at 2.7× the 16-hour allotment

Happi Planet is allotted 16 development hours a month. Across May, June, July and August we averaged 42.84 hours a month, more than double the allotment, with not one month coming in at or under 16. This document lays out the monthly numbers, where the hours went, and two retainer structures. We recommend the 40-hour structure: it is what we need to A/B test the sections we build, alongside the build work itself, and at the rate you are already consuming it is the cheaper of the two.

07 · Retainer Proposals

Structure A keeps the commitment lower and bills the overflow. Structure B commits the hours up front as a block. They are genuinely different shapes, and which one is cheaper depends entirely on monthly consumption.

Where the two structures cross over

Structure A costs ₹36,000 plus ₹1,800 for every hour past 30. Overflow carries a premium over the ₹1,200 base rate because unplanned work displaces scheduled work. Structure B is ₹48,000 flat. That puts the break-even at 36 hours 40 minutes a month: below it A is cheaper, above it B is. Our consumption sits well above it.

We recommend Structure B · 40 hours
Reason 01 · It buys the testing we cannot do today
A/B testing the sections we build needs the 40-hour allotment
This is the real argument for 40 hours, and it is about what you get rather than what we are owed. At 16 hours, recurring work absorbs the allotment twice over, so testing never gets scheduled, and across four months, not one of the 23 task lines was an A/B test. The 40-hour structure carves out 7 hours a month for exactly that: one live test at a time, run on a section we have built, with the better-performing version kept and the other retired. It is the difference between shipping a section because it was asked for and shipping the version the data supports. At 16 hours you are buying our build capacity and none of our testing.
The line that is currently zero
Nothing has been tested
Across four months and 23 task lines, not one hour went to an A/B test. We have built the FBT modules, the bundle tiers, the pricing logic and the offer placements, and not one of them has been tested against the version it replaced. That is not an oversight. At 16 hours a month, recurring work consumes the allotment twice over before testing can be scheduled at all.

Reason 02 · Consumption to date
42.84h a month, every month, for four months
40 hours is the closest tier below what we have actually delivered. Every month of the engagement cleared 30 hours except August, which landed exactly on it. So under Structure A, three of the last four months would have triggered an overflow bill and an approval conversation.
Reason 03 · The workstreams already in flight
Four live builds, none of them finished
Section 04 shows four workstreams still running: reviews, merchandising, bundles and the FBT rollout, each of which has already consumed more than two months of allotted hours. None is complete. The 40-hour block is what keeps all four moving while leaving room to test.
Reason 04 · It is the cheaper structure
₹11,112 a month less than Structure A
At 42.84 hours a month, Structure A bills ₹59,112 against Structure B's ₹48,000, a saving of ₹1,33,344 a year. Because overflow carries the ₹1,800 premium, Structure A is only cheaper if consumption falls back below 36 hours 40 minutes, and nothing in four months of data suggests it will.
Reason 05 · It removes the monthly negotiation
One fixed number, no approval cycle
A committed block means requests get scheduled on merit and sequence rather than against a ceiling. You get a predictable ₹48,000 line item, we get the headroom to keep turnaround where it is, and neither side spends the last week of the month arguing about scope.
Why we are not asking for more than 40
42.84 hours a month is what we have actually run, so 40 is deliberately set below our own track record. We would rather commit to a number we can hold consistently and quote separately for a genuinely exceptional build, as June's Lambda work would have been, than pad the retainer against a peak that may not repeat.
If you would genuinely prefer the lower commitment, Structure A is a fair offer and we will honour it, but on four months of evidence it will cost you more, not less. Structure A is the right choice only if you expect the roadmap to slow to under 36 hours 40 minutes a month, and four months of data is our honest view that it will not.
